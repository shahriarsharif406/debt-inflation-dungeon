# Debt & Inflation Dungeon: Game Budget and Production Plan

*Zoud GameLab 2026 · funding request for taking the jam build to a public release · all amounts in USD*

*The jam page does not state a per-entry funding cap. This plan requests **$48,000** over **six months**, sized for a solo lead developer plus contractors; each line scales by its share.*

## 1. What exists today

The jam build is complete and playable in a browser: 12-month game, 3 income levels, 4 assets including a space-tech Orbit Fund, budgeting and savings systems, insurance, a Daily Dungeon, XP and levels, a 21-theory Money Codex, a real-time 3D arena across three floors, a retirement account with employer match and tax relief, a credit score, scam alerts, a "what could have been" report card, a Gulf edition (AED, Islamic finance, takaful, zakat), an accessibility panel, and an Arabic interface with right-to-left layout. Balance is checked by thousands of automated games. **The funding pays for expansion, validation and reach, not for a first version.**

## 4. Why this is realistic

- **Small scope, proven core.** One codebase with no engine dependency, already tested and stable. The risk is low because the hard part exists.
- **Cheap to run.** It is a static web game. Hosting is about $100 a year, with no server and no personal data stored.
- **Scalable content.** New regions are data packs (events, names, products), so each new country costs days rather than months.

## 5. Commercial potential and sustainability

| Path | Idea | Rough potential |
|---|---|---|
| Free for players, funded by partners | Banks, regulators and charities sponsor a regional pack and a classroom licence | Core revenue |
| Teacher and school licence | Class dashboard, results summary, lesson plans | Recurring, low support cost |
| Workplace financial wellbeing | Employers offer the game as a short training in place of a dry course | Higher price per seat |
| Premium extras | Extra careers, cosmetic knights and monsters, no ads and no pay-to-win | Optional, small |

Targets after release (honest and modest): **10,000 plays in the first 3 months, 5 partner classrooms, and one sponsored regional pack.** These are goals, not promises.

## 6. Risks and how they are handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| The model is a simplification | The game says so, teaches ideas not advice, and has a native-speaker finance review for each region |
| Learning claims | Report only what players answer before and after play, with no inflated claims |
| Balance drift when adding content | The automated simulation runs on every change and must keep "idle loses, smart wins" |
| Solo-developer risk | Contractors for art, sound and translation; all source in a public repository with documents |
| Low reach | Teacher mode, regional packs, and the Daily Dungeon give a reason to return |

## 7. What success looks like at month six

A public release in three languages, a classroom guide, a small independent evaluation of what players learned, and at least one partner using it with real learners.
